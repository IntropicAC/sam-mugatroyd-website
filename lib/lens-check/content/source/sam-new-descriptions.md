**The Hesitant Believer**
*You are someone who finds it considerably easier to believe in the potential of others than your own.*
You are thoughtful, capable and often generous in the way you see the people around you. You can recognise what someone else brings to a room, encourage them when they are struggling and see the potential in them long before they can see it themselves. You understand that people make mistakes, that nobody gets everything right and that either all or none of us should be judged by our darkest days. You can offer that understanding to others quite naturally, but when it comes to applying it to yourself you feel a resistance.
The tension is that you have made yourself the exception to the understanding you extend so freely to everyone else. When something goes well, you find reasons it might not count. When someone recognises your contribution, you wonder whether they have seen the full picture. When you get something wrong, you can turn a perfectly ordinary human experience into a much bigger question about whether you are capable enough, talented enough or deserving enough to be where you are. You are not necessarily lacking confidence in every area of your life but it is more that you struggle to let the evidence of your own worth become something you can actually trust.
Over time, you can become so accustomed to questioning yourself that even the things you do well begin to feel like things you have somehow gotten away with. You keep working, keep improving and keep trying to become someone you can finally believe in, without realising that the person you are trying to prove yourself to is the one who keeps moving the finish line.
*The next step is to start looking in, actually recognising the good inside of you and realise that if given the choice you would not choose to be anyone else.*

**The Waymaker**
*You are someone who has taken the time to understand what matters to you and is increasingly building a life that reflects it.*
You have developed a clearer sense of who you are, what you value and what you want your life to look like. You are thoughtful about the decisions you make and increasingly comfortable choosing a path that feels right for you, even when it does not look like the one everyone else is following. You have a growing ability to distinguish between what you genuinely want and what you have been told you should want, and that understanding is beginning to show in the way you live. You are not necessarily someone who has everything figured out, but you are becoming someone who knows how to find your own way.
The tension is that finding your own way is not a one-time achievement. The life you build today may not be the life that fits you in a few years, and the values, ambitions and priorities that have brought you to this point may evolve as you do. It can be easy to become attached to a version of yourself that you have worked hard to understand, or to assume that because things feel aligned now, they will continue to do so without the same attention and honesty that got you here. There is a difference between knowing your own direction and assuming you no longer need to question it.
The opportunity is to trust yourself enough to keep moving, even when the path ahead is not entirely clear. You do not need to follow someone else's blueprint, nor do you need to keep searching for something to fix simply because growth is supposed to be constant. You have the foundations to make decisions that are genuinely your own. The next step is to keep using them to create a life that feels meaningful, rather than one that simply looks right from the outside.
*You have spent time learning how to find your own way. The only thing left to do now is not lose that self-trust that got you here and dig deeper within whenever the opportunity arises.*
